Lecture 4

Copies and moves

Lecture 3 found the state of part of a register. This lecture asks: what can be done with an unknown quantum state? It cannot be copied (Theorem 4.1). It can be moved: a Bell pair, a Bell-basis measurement and two bits teleport a qubit, leaving nothing behind that depends on it (Theorem 4.3, the main result); nothing about it reaches the receiver before either bit (Theorem 4.4). And a reading of wires used afterwards only as controls can wait until the end (Theorem 4.5).

By the end of this lecture you will be able to:

  • Prove that no gate clones two states unless they are orthogonal or equal up to a phase, and show that a CNOT copies ∣0⟩\ket{0}∣0⟩ and ∣1⟩\ket{1}∣1⟩ but not ∣+⟩\ket{+}∣+⟩.
  • Compute the teleportation circuit: each outcome has probability 14\tfrac1441​, and Zm1Xm2Z^{m_1}X^{m_2}Zm1​Xm2​, with XXX first, returns the input exactly.
  • Prove that before either bit arrives the receiver’s qubit is in 121\tfrac12\Id21​1, whatever the input.
  • Prove that a reading followed by gates it controls gives the same joint statistics as the controlled gates followed by the reading, and that the hypothesis is needed.

4.1 No-cloning

Lecture 1 copied bits freely: CNOT(a,0)=(a,a)\CNOT(a,0)=(a,a)CNOT(a,0)=(a,a) in (1.7) is FAN, and Bennett’s construction copies its answer before it uncomputes (Example 1.10). Is there a gate that takes an unknown state ψ\psiψ and a blank register to two copies of ψ\psiψ? It may use a workspace, and leave in it whatever it likes.

Theorem 4.1 (No-cloning). Let AAA and BBB be registers of nnn qubits each and EEE a further register; let ω\omegaω be a state of BEBEBE and UUU a gate on ABEABEABE. If two states ψ\psiψ and φ\varphiφ of AAA are cloned by UUU, that is, U(ψ⊗ω)=ψ⊗ψ⊗eψ,U(φ⊗ω)=φ⊗φ⊗eφ\begin{aligned} U(\psi\otimes\omega)&=\psi\otimes\psi\otimes e_\psi,\\ U(\varphi\otimes\omega)&=\varphi\otimes\varphi\otimes e_\varphi \end{aligned}U(ψ⊗ω)U(φ⊗ω)​=ψ⊗ψ⊗eψ​,=φ⊗φ⊗eφ​​ for some states eψe_\psieψ​ and eφe_\varphieφ​ of EEE, then ∣⟨ψ|φ⟩∣\abs{\braket{\psi}{\varphi}}∣⟨ψ∣φ⟩∣ is 000 or 111: the two states are orthogonal, or φ=eiγψ\varphi=e^{i\gamma}\psiφ=eiγψ for a real γ\gammaγ.

Proof. A gate keeps inner products: ⟨Uv|Uw⟩=⟨v∣U†U∣w⟩=⟨v|w⟩\braket{Uv}{Uw}=\bra{v}U^\dagger U\ket{w}=\braket{v}{w}⟨Uv∣Uw⟩=⟨v∣U†U∣w⟩=⟨v∣w⟩. By (3.1), the two inputs have the inner product ⟨ψ|φ⟩⟨ω|ω⟩=⟨ψ|φ⟩\braket{\psi}{\varphi}\braket{\omega}{\omega}=\braket{\psi}{\varphi}⟨ψ∣φ⟩⟨ω∣ω⟩=⟨ψ∣φ⟩, and the two outputs ⟨ψ|φ⟩2⟨eψ|eφ⟩\braket{\psi}{\varphi}^{2}\braket{e_\psi}{e_\varphi}⟨ψ∣φ⟩2⟨eψ​∣eφ​⟩. So c=∣⟨ψ|φ⟩∣c=\abs{\braket{\psi}{\varphi}}c=∣⟨ψ∣φ⟩∣ has c=c2∣⟨eψ|eφ⟩∣≤c2c=c^{2}\abs{\braket{e_\psi}{e_\varphi}}\le c^{2}c=c2∣⟨eψ​∣eφ​⟩∣≤c2, by the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, and 0≤c≤10\le c\le10≤c≤1 by the same inequality; hence ccc is 000 or 111. If c=1c=1c=1, put λ=⟨ψ|φ⟩\lambda=\braket{\psi}{\varphi}λ=⟨ψ∣φ⟩: then ∥φ−λψ∥2=1−∣λ∣2=0\norm{\varphi-\lambda\psi}^{2}=1-\abs{\lambda}^{2}=0∥φ−λψ∥2=1−∣λ∣2=0, so φ=λψ\varphi=\lambda\psiφ=λψ with ∣λ∣=1\abs{\lambda}=1∣λ∣=1. 

The workspace EEE may hold whatever a circuit of gates uses, and ω\omegaω may be entangled. A gate on ABABAB alone that clones up to a phase, U(ψ⊗s)=eiαψ⊗ψU(\psi\otimes s)=e^{i\alpha}\psi\otimes\psiU(ψ⊗s)=eiαψ⊗ψ, is the case of EEE idle in a state eee, with eψ=eiαee_\psi=e^{i\alpha}eeψ​=eiαe. Cloning is a property of rays: if UUU clones ψ\psiψ, it clones eiγψe^{i\gamma}\psieiγψ, with e−iγeψe^{-i\gamma}e_\psie−iγeψ​. That is why the conclusion is about ∣⟨ψ|φ⟩∣\abs{\braket{\psi}{\varphi}}∣⟨ψ∣φ⟩∣. Nielsen and Chuang (Box 12.1, p. 532) ask for ψ⊗ψ\psi\otimes\psiψ⊗ψ exactly and conclude ⟨ψ|φ⟩∈{0,1}\braket{\psi}{\varphi}\in\{0,1\}⟨ψ∣φ⟩∈{0,1}; read so, no gate clones both ψ\psiψ and −ψ-\psi−ψ, which are one state (Theorem 3.7).

In particular, no gate clones both ∣0⟩\ket{0}∣0⟩ and ∣+⟩\ket{+}∣+⟩, since ⟨0|+⟩=1/2\braket{0}{+}=1/\sqrt2⟨0∣+⟩=1/2​, so none clones every state of a qubit. That weaker statement is what Griffiths and Schroeter ((12.41)–(12.45)) and Watrous (p. 87) prove: by linearity, a machine that clones two states sends their superpositions to the wrong vectors. Griffiths and Schroeter motivate it by relativity, since a cloner would let a measurement on one particle of an entangled pair send a message faster than light (p. 459). The proof above uses no relativity, only that gates keep inner products. The alternative “orthogonal” does occur, as the next section shows.

4.2 The CNOT guard

Example 4.2 (QQ: CNOT is no copier). On a blank target the CNOT is FAN: CNOT∣j⟩∣0⟩=∣j⟩∣j⟩\CNOT\ket{j}\ket{0}=\ket{j}\ket{j}CNOT∣j⟩∣0⟩=∣j⟩∣j⟩. On ψ=a∣0⟩+b∣1⟩\psi=a\ket{0}+b\ket{1}ψ=a∣0⟩+b∣1⟩, by linearity, (4.1) an equality of vectors, while ψ⊗ψ=a2∣00⟩+ab∣01⟩+ab∣10⟩+b2∣11⟩\psi\otimes\psi=a^{2}\ket{00}+ab\ket{01}+ab\ket{10}+b^{2}\ket{11}ψ⊗ψ=a2∣00⟩+ab∣01⟩+ab∣10⟩+b2∣11⟩. The amplitude of ∣01⟩\ket{01}∣01⟩ is 000 on the left, so the CNOT clones ψ\psiψ, even up to a phase, only if ab=0ab=0ab=0: on the rays of ∣0⟩\ket{0}∣0⟩ and ∣1⟩\ket{1}∣1⟩. On ∣+⟩\ket{+}∣+⟩ it makes Φ+\BellΦ+ (Example 3.11), which is entangled, and whose halves are in 121\tfrac12\Id21​1, not in ∣+⟩\ket{+}∣+⟩.

So the CNOT copies a basis, as FAN does; on Lecture 1’s fair coin FAN gives 000000 or 111111, not two independent coins. Lecture 10 encodes ψ\psiψ as a∣000⟩+b∣111⟩a\ket{000}+b\ket{111}a∣000⟩+b∣111⟩ by two such CNOTs (Nielsen and Chuang, p. 427): an encoding, not three copies.

4.3 Teleportation

A state cannot be copied; it can be moved. Alice holds a qubit in an unknown state ψ\psiψ, and shares with Bob the Bell state Φ+\BellΦ+, made beforehand by the circuit (3.6), one qubit each. She can send Bob bits.

Theorem 4.3 (Teleportation). Let wire 1 carry a qubit in a state ψ\psiψ, and wires 2–3 the Bell state Φ+\BellΦ+; wires 1 and 2 are Alice’s, wire 3 is Bob’s. Alice applies a CNOT from wire 1 to wire 2 and then HHH to wire 1, and reads wires 1 and 2, with the outcomes m1m_1m1​ and m2m_2m2​; Bob applies Xm2X^{m_2}Xm2​ and then Zm1Z^{m_1}Zm1​ to wire 3: Then, for every ψ\psiψ:

  1. each pair m1m2m_1m_2m1​m2​ has probability 14\tfrac1441​;

  2. the reading leaves wires 1–2 in ∣m1m2⟩\ket{m_1m_2}∣m1​m2​⟩, and Bob’s gates, the operator Zm1Xm2Z^{m_1}X^{m_2}Zm1​Xm2​, leave wire 3 in ψ\psiψ exactly.

Proof. Write ψ=a∣0⟩+b∣1⟩\psi=a\ket{0}+b\ket{1}ψ=a∣0⟩+b∣1⟩. Then ψ⊗Φ+=(a∣000⟩+a∣011⟩+b∣100⟩+b∣111⟩)/2\psi\otimes\Bell=(a\ket{000}+a\ket{011}+b\ket{100}+b\ket{111})/\sqrt2ψ⊗Φ+=(a∣000⟩+a∣011⟩+b∣100⟩+b∣111⟩)/2​; the CNOT turns the last two terms into b∣110⟩+b∣101⟩b\ket{110}+b\ket{101}b∣110⟩+b∣101⟩, and HHH on wire 1 sends ∣0x⟩\ket{0x}∣0x⟩ to (∣0x⟩+∣1x⟩)/2(\ket{0x}+\ket{1x})/\sqrt2(∣0x⟩+∣1x⟩)/2​ and ∣1x⟩\ket{1x}∣1x⟩ to (∣0x⟩−∣1x⟩)/2(\ket{0x}-\ket{1x})/\sqrt2(∣0x⟩−∣1x⟩)/2​. Collected by the strings m1m2m_1m_2m1​m2​ of wires 1–2, the state is 12∣00⟩(a∣0⟩+b∣1⟩)+12∣01⟩(a∣1⟩+b∣0⟩)+12∣10⟩(a∣0⟩−b∣1⟩)+12∣11⟩(a∣1⟩−b∣0⟩).\begin{aligned} &\tfrac12\ket{00}(a\ket{0}+b\ket{1})\\ {}+{}&\tfrac12\ket{01}(a\ket{1}+b\ket{0})\\ {}+{}&\tfrac12\ket{10}(a\ket{0}-b\ket{1})\\ {}+{}&\tfrac12\ket{11}(a\ket{1}-b\ket{0}). \end{aligned}+++​21​∣00⟩(a∣0⟩+b∣1⟩)21​∣01⟩(a∣1⟩+b∣0⟩)21​∣10⟩(a∣0⟩−b∣1⟩)21​∣11⟩(a∣1⟩−b∣0⟩).​ Since a∣1⟩+b∣0⟩=Xψa\ket{1}+b\ket{0}=X\psia∣1⟩+b∣0⟩=Xψ, a∣0⟩−b∣1⟩=Zψa\ket{0}-b\ket{1}=Z\psia∣0⟩−b∣1⟩=Zψ and a∣1⟩−b∣0⟩=XZψa\ket{1}-b\ket{0}=XZ\psia∣1⟩−b∣0⟩=XZψ, this says (4.2) an equality of vectors. Reading wires 1–2 reads part of the register: by (3.2) it gives m1m2m_1m_2m1​m2​ with probability ∥12Xm2Zm1ψ∥2=14\norm{\tfrac12X^{m_2}Z^{m_1}\psi}^{2}=\tfrac14​21​Xm2​Zm1​ψ​2=41​, since XXX and ZZZ are unitary, and leaves ∣m1m2⟩⊗Xm2Zm1ψ\ket{m_1m_2}\otimes X^{m_2}Z^{m_1}\psi∣m1​m2​⟩⊗Xm2​Zm1​ψ. Bob’s gates make this ∣m1m2⟩⊗Zm1Xm2Xm2Zm1ψ=∣m1m2⟩⊗ψ\ket{m_1m_2}\otimes Z^{m_1}X^{m_2}X^{m_2}Z^{m_1}\psi=\ket{m_1m_2}\otimes\psi∣m1​m2​⟩⊗Zm1​Xm2​Xm2​Zm1​ψ=∣m1​m2​⟩⊗ψ, since X2=Z2=1X^{2}=Z^{2}=\IdX2=Z2=1. 

Alice’s part is a measurement in the Bell basis. With UUU the circuit of (3.6), HHH and then a CNOT, U†U^\daggerU† is a CNOT and then HHH; so by Proposition 3.6 her CNOT, HHH and reading give m1m2m_1m_2m1​m2​ with the probability of the measurement of wires 1–2 in the basis {U∣m1m2⟩}\{U\ket{m_1m_2}\}{U∣m1​m2​⟩} of the four Bell states, of which U∣00⟩=Φ+U\ket{00}=\BellU∣00⟩=Φ+. Without the final UUU, they leave ∣m1m2⟩\ket{m_1m_2}∣m1​m2​⟩: Alice’s qubits end in a computational-basis state, not in a Bell state (the circuit of Nielsen and Chuang’s Ex. 4.33).

What Alice is left with, the bits m1m2m_1m_2m1​m2​ and the state ∣m1m2⟩\ket{m_1m_2}∣m1​m2​⟩, has the same distribution for every ψ\psiψ: nothing that depends on the input stays with her. Nothing travels to Bob but the two bits; his own qubit, wire 3, ends in ψ\psiψ. The order of his gates matters: Xm2Zm1X^{m_2}Z^{m_1}Xm2​Zm1​, ZZZ first, would leave Xm2Zm1Xm2Zm1ψ=(−1)m1m2ψX^{m_2}Z^{m_1}X^{m_2}Z^{m_1}\psi=(-1)^{m_1m_2}\psiXm2​Zm1​Xm2​Zm1​ψ=(−1)m1​m2​ψ, the right ray (Theorem 3.7) but −ψ-\psi−ψ on the outcome 111111. Nielsen and Chuang (pp. 26–28) use our labels: their outcome 010101, which calls for XXX, is m1=0m_1=0m1​=0, m2=1m_2=1m2​=1. Watrous (pp. 96–101) writes kets in the order Bob, Alice’s half, input, and his outcome pair ababab is our m2m1m_2m_1m2​m1​: his 010101 calls for ZZZ.

4.4 No signalling

Bob’s gates need m1m2m_1m_2m1​m2​, and the two bits reach him no faster than light. Can he learn anything about ψ\psiψ from his own qubit before either arrives?

Theorem 4.4 (No signalling in teleportation). In the circuit of Theorem 4.3, until Bob learns either bit, his qubit is in the reduced state 121\tfrac12\Id21​1 at every step, for every input ψ\psiψ: at the start, after Alice’s CNOT, after her HHH, and after her reading.

Proof. After the HHH the register is in the state χ\chiχ of (4.2), which is ∑m∣m1m2⟩⊗χm\sum_m\ket{m_1m_2}\otimes\chi_m∑m​∣m1​m2​⟩⊗χm​ with χm=12Xm2Zm1ψ\chi_m=\tfrac12X^{m_2}Z^{m_1}\psiχm​=21​Xm2​Zm1​ψ. By Corollary 3.10, with the parts exchanged (Alice’s wires traced out in the basis ∣m1m2⟩\ket{m_1m_2}∣m1​m2​⟩), Bob’s state is ρB=∑m∣χm⟩⟨χm∣=14∑mXm2Zm1ρZm1Xm2,\begin{aligned} \rho_B&=\textstyle\sum_m\ket{\chi_m}\bra{\chi_m}\\ &=\tfrac14\textstyle\sum_mX^{m_2}Z^{m_1}\rho Z^{m_1}X^{m_2}, \end{aligned}ρB​​=∑m​∣χm​⟩⟨χm​∣=41​∑m​Xm2​Zm1​ρZm1​Xm2​,​ with ρ=∣ψ⟩⟨ψ∣\rho=\ket{\psi}\bra{\psi}ρ=∣ψ⟩⟨ψ∣. For a 2×22\times22×2 matrix ρ\rhoρ, 12(ρ+ZρZ)\tfrac12(\rho+Z\rho Z)21​(ρ+ZρZ) is its diagonal DDD, and 12(D+XDX)\tfrac12(D+XDX)21​(D+XDX) is 12(Tr⁡D)1\tfrac12(\Tr D)\Id21​(TrD)1; so the sum is 12(Tr⁡ρ)1=121\tfrac12(\Tr\rho)\Id=\tfrac12\Id21​(Trρ)1=21​1. After the reading, Bob’s qubit is in Xm2Zm1ψX^{m_2}Z^{m_1}\psiXm2​Zm1​ψ with probability 14\tfrac1441​ for each m1m2m_1m_2m1​m2​; knowing neither bit, he holds the mixture of these states, which is the same matrix. Before the HHH, and before the CNOT, the register is in WχW\chiWχ for a gate W=V⊗1W=V\otimes\IdW=V⊗1 with VVV on wires 1–2. For every 2×22\times22×2 matrix MMM, the interchange law gives W†(1⊗M)W=V†V⊗M=1⊗MW^\dagger(\Id\otimes M)W=V^\dagger V\otimes M=\Id\otimes MW†(1⊗M)W=V†V⊗M=1⊗M, so Tr⁡(WσW†(1⊗M))=Tr⁡(σ(1⊗M))\Tr\bigl(W\sigma W^\dagger(\Id\otimes M)\bigr)=\Tr\bigl(\sigma(\Id\otimes M)\bigr)Tr(WσW†(1⊗M))=Tr(σ(1⊗M)) for σ=∣χ⟩⟨χ∣\sigma=\ket{\chi}\bra{\chi}σ=∣χ⟩⟨χ∣, and by the uniqueness in Theorem 3.9 Bob’s state is unchanged. 

So before either bit arrives, every measurement of Bob’s qubit has the statistics of 121\tfrac12\Id21​1, the same for every ψ\psiψ: in every basis, each outcome has probability 12\tfrac1221​ (§3.5). Nothing about ψ\psiψ reaches Bob before a bit does. The bits alone carry nothing about ψ\psiψ either: by Theorem 4.3 (i) they are uniform for every ψ\psiψ. Only together with Bob’s qubit do they give ψ\psiψ. Nielsen and Chuang (p. 108) compute 121\tfrac12\Id21​1 after Alice’s reading; it holds at every step.

4.5 Deferred measurement

Bob’s gates are classically controlled: which one acts depends on the outcome of a reading. Can the reading wait until the end?

Theorem 4.5 (Deferred measurement). Let ABABAB be a register, with AAA of mmm qubits, and for each k∈{0,1}mk\in\{0,1\}^{m}k∈{0,1}m let UkU_kUk​ be a gate on BBB; put G=∑k∣k⟩⟨k∣⊗UkG=\sum_k\ket{k}\bra{k}\otimes U_kG=∑k​∣k⟩⟨k∣⊗Uk​. On every state χ\chiχ of ABABAB the procedures

  1. read AAA, then apply UkU_kUk​ to BBB, where kkk is the outcome:

  2. apply GGG, then read AAA:

give each outcome kkk with the same probability pkp_kpk​ and, if pk>0p_k>0pk​>0, leave the same state. So all later steps give the same joint statistics.

Proof. GGG is a gate, since G†G=∑k∣k⟩⟨k∣⊗Uk†Uk=1G^\dagger G=\sum_k\ket{k}\bra{k}\otimes U_k^\dagger U_k=\IdG†G=∑k​∣k⟩⟨k∣⊗Uk†​Uk​=1. Let Πk=∣k⟩⟨k∣⊗1\Proj_k=\ket{k}\bra{k}\otimes\IdΠk​=∣k⟩⟨k∣⊗1 be the projectors of the reading of AAA; by the interchange law, GΠk=∣k⟩⟨k∣⊗Uk=ΠkGG\Proj_k=\ket{k}\bra{k}\otimes U_k=\Proj_kGGΠk​=∣k⟩⟨k∣⊗Uk​=Πk​G. In (a), the reading gives kkk with pk=∥Πkχ∥2p_k=\norm{\Proj_k\chi}^{2}pk​=∥Πk​χ∥2 and, if pk>0p_k>0pk​>0, leaves Πkχ/pk=∣k⟩⊗φk\Proj_k\chi/\sqrt{p_k}=\ket{k}\otimes\varphi_kΠk​χ/pk​​=∣k⟩⊗φk​ by (3.2); then UkU_kUk​ makes it ∣k⟩⊗Ukφk=G(∣k⟩⊗φk)\ket{k}\otimes U_k\varphi_k=G(\ket{k}\otimes\varphi_k)∣k⟩⊗Uk​φk​=G(∣k⟩⊗φk​). In (b), the reading gives kkk with ∥ΠkGχ∥2=∥GΠkχ∥2=pk\norm{\Proj_kG\chi}^{2}=\norm{G\Proj_k\chi}^{2}=p_k∥Πk​Gχ∥2=∥GΠk​χ∥2=pk​, since GGG is unitary, and, if pk>0p_k>0pk​>0, leaves ΠkGχ/pk=GΠkχ/pk=G(∣k⟩⊗φk)\Proj_kG\chi/\sqrt{p_k}=G\Proj_k\chi/\sqrt{p_k}=G(\ket{k}\otimes\varphi_k)Πk​Gχ/pk​​=GΠk​χ/pk​​=G(∣k⟩⊗φk​), as (a) does. 

After the reading, AAA holds ∣k⟩\ket{k}∣k⟩, on which a gate ∑j∣j⟩⟨j∣⊗Vj\sum_j\ket{j}\bra{j}\otimes V_j∑j​∣j⟩⟨j∣⊗Vj​, one that touches AAA only as a control, acts as VkV_kVk​: as the gate classically controlled by the outcome. Gates on BBB alone have this form, as do controlled gates with the control in AAA and the target in BBB, and products of these. So if every step after a reading is such a gate, the reading can be moved to the end, and classically controlled gates become controlled ones.

The hypothesis bites: HHH does not commute with the Πk\Proj_kΠk​. Read ∣+⟩\ket{+}∣+⟩, apply HHH and read again: 000 and 111, with probability 12\tfrac1221​ each. Without the first reading, H∣+⟩=∣0⟩H\ket{+}=\ket{0}H∣+⟩=∣0⟩ reads 000 with certainty (Theorem 3.7). The runs share joint statistics, not states: before its reading, (b) holds a superposition of the branches of (a). Nielsen and Chuang’s principle (p. 186) omits the hypothesis; their Ex. 4.35 (p. 188) has it.

Example 4.6 (QQ plus an input: teleportation, deferred). Theorem 4.3 is the case A=A=A= wires 1–2, B=B=B= wire 3, Um1m2=Zm1Xm2U_{m_1m_2}=Z^{m_1}X^{m_2}Um1​m2​​=Zm1​Xm2​: GGG is a CNOT from wire 2 to wire 3, then a controlled ZZZ from wire 1 to wire 3, which acts as Zm1Z^{m_1}Zm1​ on wire 3. By (4.2) it takes χ\chiχ to 12∑m∣m1m2⟩⊗ψ\tfrac12\sum_m\ket{m_1m_2}\otimes\psi21​∑m​∣m1​m2​⟩⊗ψ: (4.3) an equality of vectors. Read at the end, wires 1–2 give each m1m2m_1m_2m1​m2​ with probability 14\tfrac1441​, and wire 3 holds ψ\psiψ. It is no protocol for Alice and Bob apart: its last two gates join her wires to his; Lecture 6 uses this form for the coins of a probabilistic algorithm.

Summary

  • Copies: no gate clones two states unless they are orthogonal or one ray (Theorem 4.1); a CNOT copies ∣0⟩\ket{0}∣0⟩ and ∣1⟩\ket{1}∣1⟩, not ∣+⟩\ket{+}∣+⟩ (Example 4.2).
  • The main result: a Bell pair, a Bell-basis reading and two bits teleport a qubit; each outcome has probability 14\tfrac1441​, and Zm1Xm2Z^{m_1}X^{m_2}Zm1​Xm2​ returns the input exactly (Theorem 4.3).
  • No signalling: until Bob learns either bit, his qubit is in 121\tfrac12\Id21​1 (Theorem 4.4).
  • Deferred measurement: a reading of wires used afterwards only as controls moves to the end, with the same joint statistics (Theorem 4.5); Lecture 6 uses it.
