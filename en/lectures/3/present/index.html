Lecture 3

Registers: the state of a part

Lecture 2 read a whole register, and only at the end of a circuit. This lecture reads part of one, and asks: what is the state of part of a register? The answer, the reduced state (Theorem 3.9), is the one matrix that gives every measurement of the part the statistics the whole register gives; it has a formula in any basis. For the Bell state the answer is 121\tfrac12\Id21​1, which no ket describes.

By the end of this lecture you will be able to:

  • Build a register from its parts as a tensor product, and decide whether a state of two qubits is a product.
  • Compute the probabilities and the states left by a reading of part of a register, and by a measurement in any basis, drawn as a circuit.
  • Prove that a global phase changes nothing in any procedure, while a relative phase can be seen.
  • Define the reduced state by its property, prove it unique, derive its formula, and compute it for the Bell state.

3.1 Tensor structure and entanglement

Let a register ABABAB of m+nm+nm+n qubits have the part AAA on its top mmm wires and the part BBB on the nnn wires below. For columns ψ∈C2m\psi\in\C^{2^{m}}ψ∈C2m and φ∈C2n\varphi\in\C^{2^{n}}φ∈C2n, the Kronecker product of Definition 1.2 is the column ψ⊗φ∈C2m+n\psi\otimes\varphi\in\C^{2^{m+n}}ψ⊗φ∈C2m+n with the entries (ψ⊗φ)xx′=ψxφx′,(\psi\otimes\varphi)_{xx'}=\psi_x\varphi_{x'},(ψ⊗φ)xx′​=ψx​φx′​, one for each string xx′xx'xx′ of ABABAB; it is linear in each factor, and is also written ∣ψ⟩∣φ⟩\ket{\psi}\ket{\varphi}∣ψ⟩∣φ⟩. By the interchange law and (2.3), ⟨ψ⊗φ|ψ′⊗φ′⟩=(ψ†⊗φ†)(ψ′⊗φ′)=⟨ψ|ψ′⟩⟨φ|φ′⟩, \begin{aligned} \braket{\psi\otimes\varphi}{\psi'\otimes\varphi'} &=(\psi^\dagger\otimes\varphi^\dagger)(\psi'\otimes\varphi')\\ &=\braket{\psi}{\psi'}\braket{\varphi}{\varphi'}, \end{aligned} ⟨ψ⊗φ∣ψ′⊗φ′⟩​=(ψ†⊗φ†)(ψ′⊗φ′)=⟨ψ∣ψ′⟩⟨φ∣φ′⟩,​(3.1) so a product of states is a state, and (U⊗V)(ψ⊗φ)=Uψ⊗Vφ(U\otimes V)(\psi\otimes\varphi)=U\psi\otimes V\varphi(U⊗V)(ψ⊗φ)=Uψ⊗Vφ: a gate on one part acts on its own factor. The columns ∣x⟩∣x′⟩=∣xx′⟩\ket{x}\ket{x'}=\ket{xx'}∣x⟩∣x′⟩=∣xx′⟩ are a basis, by (1.3), so every state of ABABAB is ∑x,x′cxx′∣x⟩∣x′⟩\sum_{x,x'}c_{xx'}\ket{x}\ket{x'}∑x,x′​cxx′​∣x⟩∣x′⟩. In the language of Linear Algebra (L12), C2m+n\C^{2^{m+n}}C2m+n is the tensor product C2m⊗C2n\C^{2^{m}}\otimes\C^{2^{n}}C2m⊗C2n, realised by Kronecker products. Not every state of ABABAB is a product.

Definition 3.1 (Product, entangled). A state of ABABAB is a product if it is ψ⊗φ\psi\otimes\varphiψ⊗φ for a state ψ\psiψ of AAA and a state φ\varphiφ of BBB; otherwise it is entangled.

Proposition 3.2 (A test for two qubits). A state ∑j,kcjk∣jk⟩\sum_{j,k}c_{jk}\ket{jk}∑j,k​cjk​∣jk⟩ of two qubits is a product if and only if c00c11=c01c10c_{00}c_{11}=c_{01}c_{10}c00​c11​=c01​c10​.

Proof. If it is ψ⊗φ\psi\otimes\varphiψ⊗φ, then cjk=ψjφkc_{jk}=\psi_j\varphi_kcjk​=ψj​φk​, and both sides are ψ0ψ1φ0φ1\psi_0\psi_1\varphi_0\varphi_1ψ0​ψ1​φ0​φ1​. Conversely, the condition says that the 2×22\times22×2 matrix (cjk)(c_{jk})(cjk​), which is not zero, has determinant zero, so it has rank one: its rows are ψ0uT\psi_0u^{\mathsf T}ψ0​uT and ψ1uT\psi_1u^{\mathsf T}ψ1​uT for one non-zero column uuu. Then cjk=ψjukc_{jk}=\psi_ju_kcjk​=ψj​uk​, and the state is ψ⊗u\psi\otimes uψ⊗u. By (3.1), ∥ψ∥∥u∥=1\norm{\psi}\norm{u}=1∥ψ∥∥u∥=1, so it is the product of the states ψ/∥ψ∥\psi/\norm{\psi}ψ/∥ψ∥ and ∥ψ∥u\norm{\psi}u∥ψ∥u. 

Example 3.3 (QQ: a product, and an entangled state). The state ∣+⟩∣+⟩=12(∣00⟩+∣01⟩+∣10⟩+∣11⟩)\ket{+}\ket{+}=\tfrac12\bigl(\ket{00}+\ket{01}+\ket{10}+\ket{11}\bigr)∣+⟩∣+⟩=21​(∣00⟩+∣01⟩+∣10⟩+∣11⟩) has c00c11=14=c01c10c_{00}c_{11}=\tfrac14=c_{01}c_{10}c00​c11​=41​=c01​c10​: it is a product, although every string appears in it. The Bell state ∣Φ+⟩=∣00⟩+∣11⟩2\ket{\Bell}=\frac{\ket{00}+\ket{11}}{\sqrt2}​Φ+⟩=2​∣00⟩+∣11⟩​ has c00c11=12c_{00}c_{11}=\tfrac12c00​c11​=21​ and c01c10=0c_{01}c_{10}=0c01​c10​=0: it is entangled.

Griffiths and Schroeter (p. 199) first describe entanglement by dependence: the state of one particle “depends on” the state of the other. Their definition, which follows, is this course’s: not a product. A superposition of every string can still be a product.

3.2 Measuring part of a register

Definition 2.1 read a whole register as Griffiths’ measurement (3.43) of an observable with 2n2^{n}2n distinct eigenvalues, each of multiplicity one. A reading of the part AAA has degenerate outcomes: the outcome kkk is shared by every string kx′kx'kx′. Griffiths’ rule does not say what state such a reading leaves; its collapse “to the corresponding eigenstate” (p. 102) presumes there is only one. The rule that does say is Lüders’.

Definition 3.4 (Projective measurement, Lüders’ rule). The probabilities add up to ⟨ψ|ψ⟩=1\braket{\psi}{\psi}=1⟨ψ∣ψ⟩=1. Example 3.5 (QQ: reading one qubit). Read the top qubit of ∣+⟩∣+⟩\ket{+}\ket{+}∣+⟩∣+⟩. The outcome 000 has probability 12\tfrac1221​, and its eigenspace, spanned by ∣00⟩\ket{00}∣00⟩ and ∣01⟩\ket{01}∣01⟩, has dimension two; Lüders’ rule leaves ∣0⟩∣+⟩\ket{0}\ket{+}∣0⟩∣+⟩, and the qubit not read is as it was. The word “collapse” names this update. It is not a dynamics: it is the rule by which the predictions for later steps are conditioned on the outcome.

Other bases. For a gate UUU on AAA, the basis {U∣k⟩}\{U\ket{k}\}{U∣k⟩} of AAA (every orthonormal basis is one) gives the projective measurement ΠkU=U∣k⟩⟨k∣U†⊗1,\Proj^{U}_k=U\ket{k}\bra{k}U^\dagger\otimes\Id,ΠkU​=U∣k⟩⟨k∣U†⊗1, the measurement of AAA in the basis {U∣k⟩}\{U\ket{k}\}{U∣k⟩}.

3.3 Phases

Lecture 2 promised that the sign (−1)f(0)(-1)^{f(0)}(−1)f(0) left by Deutsch’s circuit (Example 2.6) changes nothing, whatever follows. A procedure is a sequence of steps, each a gate, the joining of a fresh register in a given state, or a projective measurement; each step may be chosen according to the outcomes before it.

Theorem 3.7 (Global and relative phases). Let ψ\psiψ be a state and α\alphaα real.

  1. For every procedure, the runs on ψ\psiψ and on eiαψe^{i\alpha}\psieiαψ give every sequence of outcomes with the same probability, and so does a run in which a gate UUU of the procedure is replaced by eiαUe^{i\alpha}UeiαU.

  2. ∣+⟩\ket{+}∣+⟩ and ∣−⟩\ket{-}∣−⟩, read, give 000 and 111 with probability 12\tfrac1221​ each; HHH and a reading tell them apart with certainty.

Proof. (i) By induction on the number of steps, with the claim: every sequence hhh of outcomes so far has the same probability in the two runs, and if it is positive, the states it leaves are ψh\psi_hψh​ and chψhc_h\psi_hch​ψh​ with ∣ch∣=1\abs{c_h}=1∣ch​∣=1. At the start, ccc is eiαe^{i\alpha}eiα, or 111 when a gate is replaced. The step after hhh is the same in both runs, since it depends on hhh only. A gate sends chψhc_h\psi_hch​ψh​ to chUψhc_hU\psi_hch​Uψh​, and eiαUe^{i\alpha}UeiαU sends it to eiαchUψhe^{i\alpha}c_hU\psi_heiαch​Uψh​; a joined register gives ch(ψh⊗φ)c_h(\psi_h\otimes\varphi)ch​(ψh​⊗φ); a projective measurement gives kkk with the probability ∥Πkchψh∥2=∥Πkψh∥2\norm{\Proj_kc_h\psi_h}^{2}=\norm{\Proj_k\psi_h}^{2}∥Πk​ch​ψh​∥2=∥Πk​ψh​∥2, and leaves chc_hch​ times the state it leaves from ψh\psi_hψh​. (ii) The amplitudes of ∣±⟩\ket{\pm}∣±⟩ are ±1/2\pm1/\sqrt2±1/2​, and H∣+⟩=∣0⟩H\ket{+}=\ket{0}H∣+⟩=∣0⟩, H∣−⟩=∣1⟩H\ket{-}=\ket{1}H∣−⟩=∣1⟩ by (2.2), since H2=1H^{2}=\IdH2=1. 

The predictions depend on a state only through its ray, the set of its multiples eiαψe^{i\alpha}\psieiαψ, and the sign in Example 2.6 may be dropped. A relative phase, the sign of one amplitude of ∣−⟩\ket{-}∣−⟩, is seen by (ii): By (i), a gate may be replaced by eiαe^{i\alpha}eiα times itself, as U≃VU\phaseeq VU≃V (equal up to a global phase) allows; inside a controlled gate it may not, since there the phase becomes relative (Lecture 6).

3.5 The Bell state

Example 3.11 (QQ: the Bell state and its half). A Hadamard, then a CNOT, makes Φ+\BellΦ+ (Nielsen and Chuang, §1.3.6): (3.6) an equality of vectors: (H⊗1)∣00⟩=∣+⟩∣0⟩(H\otimes\Id)\ket{00}=\ket{+}\ket{0}(H⊗1)∣00⟩=∣+⟩∣0⟩, which is (∣00⟩+∣10⟩)/2(\ket{00}+\ket{10})/\sqrt2(∣00⟩+∣10⟩)/2​, and the CNOT turns ∣10⟩\ket{10}∣10⟩ into ∣11⟩\ket{11}∣11⟩. The half has no ket. In every basis {U∣k⟩}\{U\ket{k}\}{U∣k⟩} the half gives each outcome with probability Tr⁡(12U∣k⟩⟨k∣U†)=12\Tr(\tfrac12U\ket{k}\bra{k}U^\dagger)=\tfrac12Tr(21​U∣k⟩⟨k∣U†)=21​, as its reading in Example 3.5 did. Summary

  • Handed on: registers as tensor products, and entangled as not a product (Definition 3.1 and Proposition 3.2); Lüders’ rule for reading a part, and the measurement in the basis {U∣k⟩}\{U\ket{k}\}{U∣k⟩} as U†U^\daggerU†, a reading and UUU (Definition 3.4 and Proposition 3.6).
  • Phases: a global phase changes nothing in any procedure; a relative one can be seen (Theorem 3.7).
  • The main result: the state of a part is Tr⁡Bρ\Tr_B\rhoTrB​ρ, the one matrix that predicts every measurement of the part, with a formula in every basis (Theorem 3.9 and Corollary 3.10).
  • QQ: the Bell state is pure, and its halves are in 121\tfrac12\Id21​1, which no ket describes (Example 3.11).
