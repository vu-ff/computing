Lecture 1

What a computation is

The course asks one question: what is a quantum computation, and what makes the difference visible? This lecture asks the first half of it about ordinary bits, so that Lecture 2 can say exactly what changes.

By the end of this lecture you will be able to:

  • Read a circuit as a formula, and compute its matrix: products in sequence, Kronecker products in parallel.
  • Prove the interchange law, and use it to slide gates past each other.
  • Explain why a register of random bits, described by 2n2^{n}2n numbers, yields one string when it is read.
  • Build UfU_fUf​ from a classical circuit by compute, copy, uncompute, count its gates and ancillas, and say what is lost without the uncompute.

1.1 The question and the plan

A classical computation evaluates a function f ⁣:{0,1}n→{0,1}m,f\colon\{0,1\}^{n}\to\{0,1\}^{m},f:{0,1}n→{0,1}m, nnn bits in and mmm bits out, by elementary steps, each of which acts on a few bits. The sum of two kkk-bit numbers is such a function, with n=2kn=2kn=2k and m=k+1m=k+1m=k+1. The cost of a computation is its number of steps.

Quantum computers run such computations too: the oracles of Lecture 5 and the modular arithmetic of Lecture 8 are classical functions evaluated inside a quantum circuit. But a quantum gate is unitary (Lecture 2), hence invertible, and the AND of two bits is not: the answer 000 does not say which of three inputs produced it. So this lecture asks: what does it cost to make a computation reversible? Bennett’s theorem (Theorem 1.9) answers: a number of gates linear in the number of steps and of output bits, and a workspace of extra bits that must be, and can be, returned to 000.

1.5 Reversible gates

Definition 1.7 (Reversible gate). A gate is reversible if it is a bijection of {0,1}k\{0,1\}^{k}{0,1}k onto itself. Its matrix is then a permutation matrix, and Mg−1=MgTM_g^{-1}=M_g^{\mathsf T}Mg−1​=MgT​. The reversible gates of this course are NOT(a)=1⊕a,CNOT(a,b)=(a, b⊕a),Tof(a,b,c)=(a, b, c⊕ab),\begin{gathered} \mathrm{NOT}(a)=1\oplus a,\\ \CNOT(a,b)=(a,\,b\oplus a),\\ \Tof(a,b,c)=(a,\,b,\,c\oplus ab), \end{gathered}NOT(a)=1⊕a,CNOT(a,b)=(a,b⊕a),Tof(a,b,c)=(a,b,c⊕ab),​ the NOT, the controlled NOT and the Toffoli gate, drawn with ⊕\oplus⊕ on the target and dots on the controls, which are read and left unchanged. Drawn on any wires of a register, neighbours or not, a gate changes the bits on its wires as above and leaves the others alone. That fixes its matrix, and two gates on disjoint wires commute.

AND is not reversible, since 000000, 010101 and 101010 all give 000, and nor is FAN, which never outputs 010101 or 101010. Each of NOT, CNOT and Toffoli is its own inverse; for the Toffoli, since c⊕ab⊕ab=cc\oplus ab\oplus ab=cc⊕ab⊕ab=c, (1.6) as matrices. So a circuit Gk⋯G1G_k\cdots G_1Gk​⋯G1​ of these gates has the inverse G1⋯GkG_1\cdots G_kG1​⋯Gk​: the mirror image of its diagram.

So O(s+m)O(s+m)O(s+m) gates and O(s)O(s)O(s) ancillas compute UfU_fUf​ and return the ancillas clean.

The circuit is compute, copy, uncompute: where CCC and C−1C^{-1}C−1 act only on the wires of xxx and the ancillas, and the copy only writes on yyy.

Proof. Order the gates so that each comes after the gates that feed it, and give each gate an ancilla of its own. Every wire of the given circuit then has a home in the reversible one: an input wire of xxx, or the ancilla of the gate it leaves, except that the first output of a FAN keeps the home of the wire entering it. Wires that have started and not yet ended have distinct homes: a gate ends its input wires, and each new wire gets a fresh ancilla or, for that FAN output, the home of the wire just ended.

Compute. Take the gates in order. An AND gate becomes a Toffoli whose controls are the homes of its two inputs, which are distinct, and whose target is its ancilla. A NOT gate becomes a CNOT from the home of its input onto its ancilla, followed by a NOT on the ancilla, and a FAN gate a CNOT from the home of its input onto its ancilla. By (1.7) and induction along the order, the home of every wire then holds the bit that the wire carries on the input xxx: an ancilla is written only by its own gate, after the homes of that gate’s inputs. Call this circuit CCC; it has at most 2s2s2s gates. All its targets are ancillas, so it never touches yyy: for every yyy, C(x, y, 0)=(x, y, w(x)), C(x,\,y,\,0)=(x,\,y,\,w(x)), C(x,y,0)=(x,y,w(x)),(1.9) where w(x)w(x)w(x) is the string of the ancillas’ bits on the input xxx.

1.7 The garbage guard

Example 1.11 (Compute and copy, without uncomputing). Stop the construction of Example 1.10 after the copy: The output register is right and the circuit is reversible, but the ancilla now holds x1x2x_1x_2x1​x2​, which depends on xxx. So the circuit is not UAND⊗∣c⟩U_{\mathrm{AND}}\otimes\ket{c}UAND​⊗∣c⟩ for any fixed ancilla value ccc.

To a classical computer this garbage is harmless: it throws the ancilla away. The hypothesis “clean” is for Lecture 5. There the input is a superposition of many xxx, and Lecture 5 shows on an example that garbage which depends on xxx, and so tells the branches apart, destroys the interference an algorithm relies on, while garbage that is the same for every xxx changes nothing. That is why Theorem 1.9 uncomputes, and why every oracle UfU_fUf​ from Lecture 5 on is clean.

Summary

  • Handed on to every lecture: diagrams read left to right and formulas right to left, the top wire the first factor and the most significant bit; the matrix of a circuit, from products in sequence and Kronecker products in parallel, which agree by the interchange law (Theorem 1.3 and Corollary 1.4).
  • The foil: random bits obey the same rules with stochastic matrices, and nnn of them already take 2n2^{n}2n numbers to describe (Definition 1.5).
  • The main result: a function computed by sss AND, NOT and FAN gates has a circuit of at most 4s+m4s+m4s+m Toffoli, CNOT and NOT gates and at most sss ancillas that computes UfU_fUf​ and returns the ancillas to 000 (Theorem 1.9).
  • The guard: without uncomputing, the ancilla keeps garbage that depends on xxx (Example 1.11); Lecture 5 shows what that costs.
  • Next: Lecture 2 keeps these rules and replaces probabilities by amplitudes, which can cancel.
