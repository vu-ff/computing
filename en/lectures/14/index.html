Lecture 14

The toric code: topology protects

What this lecture does

Lecture 13 ended with a question: can protection grow with the size of the system? The toric code answers it. Its qubits sit on the edges of a square lattice on a torus, each star and plaquette acts on four qubits, and it encodes two qubits (Theorem 14.2). Its distance is the size LLL (Theorem 14.3). Its errors are strings, and their syndromes, at the ends, are anyons (Theorem 14.4). This is the second half of the third answer, and the door to Part II.

By the end of this lecture you will be able to:

  • Define the toric code, and prove that its stars and plaquettes commute and that it encodes k=2k=2k=2.
  • Show that contractible loops and dual loops are products of stabilizers and that the straight loops around the torus are logical, and state that d=Ld=Ld=L.
  • Prove that errors are strings whose syndromes sit at their ends, that taking an eee around an mmm gives −1-1−1, and that the logical XXX and ZZZ anticommute for the same reason.
  • Say what “protected” means: what least-weight decoding corrects on L=3L=3L=3, and how failure falls as LLL grows, under a stated model.

14.1 The lattice, commutation

Take L≥2L\ge2L≥2 and the L×LL\times LL×L square lattice on a torus: vertices (i,j)(i,j)(i,j) with i,j∈ZLi,j\in\Z_Li,j∈ZL​ (row iii, column jjj), the horizontal edge h(i,j)h(i,j)h(i,j) from (i,j)(i,j)(i,j) to (i,j+1)(i,j+1)(i,j+1), and the vertical edge v(i,j)v(i,j)v(i,j) from (i,j)(i,j)(i,j) to (i+1,j)(i+1,j)(i+1,j), with all indices modulo LLL: the right side is glued to the left, the bottom to the top. There are L2L^{2}L2 vertices, L2L^{2}L2 faces and 2L22L^{2}2L2 edges. The face p(i,j)p(i,j)p(i,j) has the edges h(i,j)h(i,j)h(i,j), h(i+1,j)h(i+1,j)h(i+1,j), v(i,j)v(i,j)v(i,j) and v(i,j+1)v(i,j+1)v(i,j+1). As L≥2L\ge2L≥2, the four edges at a vertex are distinct, so are the four of a face, and each edge has two distinct ends and borders two distinct faces.

Definition 14.1 (The toric code). Put a qubit on each of the 2L22L^{2}2L2 edges. The star of a vertex sss is As=∏e∋sXeA_s=\prod_{e\ni s}X_eAs​=∏e∋s​Xe​, over the four edges at sss; the plaquette of a face ppp is Bp=∏e∈∂pZeB_p=\prod_{e\in\partial p}Z_eBp​=∏e∈∂p​Ze​, over the four edges of its boundary. The toric code is the code C(S)C(S)C(S) of the group SSS generated by all stars and plaquettes.

The star of s=(1,1)s=(1,1)s=(1,1) and the plaquette of p=p(2,2)p=p(2,2)p=p(2,2) on L=4L=4L=4; the arrows mark the sides that are glued. Stars commute with stars, and plaquettes with plaquettes. A star and a plaquette share no edge unless sss is a corner of ppp, and then exactly the two edges of ∂p\partial p∂p at sss: they differ on 000 or 222 qubits, so they commute (§12.1). So SSS is abelian; that −1∉S-\Id\notin S−1∈/S is proved below. This is Kitaev’s definition (1997, eq. (1), p. 2), and Dennis, Kitaev, Landahl and Preskill’s (p. 4); Kitaev writes kkk, and Kitaev, Shen and Vyalyi write rrr (p. 170), for our LLL.

A set ccc of edges is a loop if every vertex meets an even number of its edges, and a dual loop if every face has an even number of them on its boundary: the dual edges, one across each edge of ccc joining the two faces at it, then form closed paths through the faces. As the stars are products of XXX’s and the plaquettes of ZZZ’s, a product X(a)Z(b)=∏e∈aXe∏e∈bZeX(a)Z(b)=\prod_{e\in a}X_e\prod_{e\in b}Z_eX(a)Z(b)=∏e∈a​Xe​∏e∈b​Ze​, times a phase, commutes with every star exactly when bbb is a loop, and with every plaquette exactly when aaa is a dual loop.

14.2 k=2k=2k=2

Theorem 14.2 (k=2k=2k=2). For L≥2L\ge2L≥2, a product of stars and plaquettes is ±1\pm\Id±1 only if it uses all the stars or none and all the plaquettes or none, and it is then 1\Id1; so −1∉S-\Id\notin S−1∈/S. Dropping one star and one plaquette leaves 2L2−22L^{2}-22L2−2 independent generators of SSS, and the toric code has dimension 444: k=2k=2k=2.

Proof. Let TTT be a set of vertices. Each edge lies in the stars of its two ends, and Xe2=1X_e^{2}=\IdXe2​=1, so ∏s∈TAs=X(a)\prod_{s\in T}A_s=X(a)∏s∈T​As​=X(a), with aaa the set of edges that have exactly one end in TTT. If aaa is empty, every neighbour of a vertex of TTT is in TTT, and as the lattice is connected, TTT is empty or all of it. Likewise, for a set QQQ of faces, ∏p∈QBp=Z(b)\prod_{p\in Q}B_p=Z(b)∏p∈Q​Bp​=Z(b), with bbb the set of edges that border exactly one face of QQQ, and bbb is empty only if QQQ is empty or all of it, as the faces are connected across edges. Stars commute with plaquettes, so ∏TAs∏QBp=X(a)Z(b)\prod_TA_s\prod_QB_p=X(a)Z(b)∏T​As​∏Q​Bp​=X(a)Z(b), in any order and with no phase. It is a multiple of 1\Id1 only if aaa and bbb are empty: a qubit in aaa or bbb carries XXX, ZZZ or XZ=−iYXZ=-iYXZ=−iY. So the only products that are ±1\pm\Id±1 are the four with TTT and QQQ each empty or all, and each is 1\Id1.

Now drop As0A_{s_0}As0​​ and Bp0B_{p_0}Bp0​​. If a remaining generator were a product of the others, a product of stars and plaquettes with s0∉Ts_0\notin Ts0​∈/T, p0∉Qp_0\notin Qp0​∈/Q and TTT or QQQ nonempty would be 1\Id1, which we excluded. And the dropped two are products of the rest, As0=∏s≠s0AsA_{s_0}=\prod_{s\neq s_0}A_sAs0​​=∏s=s0​​As​ and Bp0=∏p≠p0BpB_{p_0}=\prod_{p\neq p_0}B_pBp0​​=∏p=p0​​Bp​, so the rest still generate SSS. By Theorem 12.3 the code has dimension 22L2−(2L2−2)=42^{2L^{2}-(2L^{2}-2)}=422L2−(2L2−2)=4. 

The stars of (1,1)(1,1)(1,1) and (1,2)(1,2)(1,2) share h(1,1)h(1,1)h(1,1), which cancels: their product is XXX on the six other edges at the two vertices, the edges that the dashed dual loop around them crosses. A contractible dual loop is a product of stars.

The two relations ∏sAs=∏pBp=1\prod_sA_s=\prod_pB_p=\Id∏s​As​=∏p​Bp​=1 make the 2L22L^{2}2L2 generators dependent, as were Lecture 12’s Z1Z2Z_1Z_2Z1​Z2​, Z2Z3Z_2Z_3Z2​Z3​, Z1Z3Z_1Z_3Z1​Z3​: counting them all would give k=0k=0k=0. Kitaev (p. 3), Dennis et al. (p. 5: “There are no further relations among these operators”), Kitaev, Shen and Vyalyi (p. 171) and Watrous (2025, p. 450) state that these are the only relations; none of them proves it. The proof uses only that the lattice and its faces are connected.

14.3 Loops as logical operators; d=Ld=Ld=L

Theorem 14.3 (Logical operators (iii) cited).

  2. 14.4 Strings, anyons, −1-1−1

An error X(a)X(a)X(a) violates the plaquettes ppp that meet aaa in an odd number of edges, and Z(b)Z(b)Z(b) the stars that meet bbb in an odd number; a YYY is both. Call a violated star an eee and a violated plaquette an mmm. A path is a sequence of edges, each starting where the last ended; a dual path is a sequence of dual edges, from face to face.

Theorem 14.4 (Strings and anyons).

  1. ZZZ on a path from a vertex uuu to a vertex w≠uw\neq uw=u violates exactly the stars at uuu and www; XXX on a dual path from a face fff to a face g≠fg\neq fg=f violates exactly the plaquettes fff and ggg.

  2. Let ψ\psiψ be in the code, ttt a dual path from fff to g≠fg\neq fg=f, and QQQ a set of faces with f∈Qf\in Qf∈Q and g∉Qg\notin Qg∈/Q. Then Z(∂Q) X(t)ψ=−X(t)ψZ(\partial Q)\,X(t)\psi=-X(t)\psiZ(∂Q)X(t)ψ=−X(t)ψ.

Proof. (i) Each passage of the path through a vertex uses two edges at it, and the two ends one each; an edge used twice cancels, as Ze2=1Z_e^{2}=\IdZe2​=1. So the star of a vertex meets the path in an odd number of edges exactly at uuu and www. The dual case is the same, faces for vertices. (ii) By Theorem 14.3(i), Z(∂Q)=∏p∈QBpZ(\partial Q)=\prod_{p\in Q}B_pZ(∂Q)=∏p∈Q​Bp​, and by (i), BpB_pBp​ anticommutes with X(t)X(t)X(t) exactly for p=fp=fp=f and p=gp=gp=g; one of them is in QQQ. So Z(∂Q)X(t)=−X(t)Z(∂Q)Z(\partial Q)X(t)=-X(t)Z(\partial Q)Z(∂Q)X(t)=−X(t)Z(∂Q), and Z(∂Q)ψ=ψZ(\partial Q)\psi=\psiZ(∂Q)ψ=ψ. 

So errors are strings, and their syndromes sit at the ends: ZZZ on a path leaves two eee’s (above), XXX on a dual path two mmm’s (below). Read (ii) as a process: an eee pair is made by ZZZ on one edge of ∂Q\partial Q∂Q, one eee is taken once along ∂Q\partial Q∂Q, around the region QQQ that holds the mmm at fff, and the pair is removed; the net operator is Z(∂Q)Z(\partial Q)Z(∂Q), and the state changes sign. In the picture ∂Q\partial Q∂Q crosses the mmm-string once. The logical Xˉa\bar X_aXˉa​ and Zˉa\bar Z_aZˉa​ anticommute for the same reason: their loops cross once. This is Kitaev’s computation (§2, Fig. 4, p. 7; W=−1W=-1W=−1, p. 8); also Kitaev, Shen and Vyalyi (p. 175) and Dennis et al. (p. 9). Kitaev’s “zzz-type particles” are our eee’s.

The −1-1−1 is a monodromy: an eee taken once around an mmm. It is not the phase of an exchange, as eee and mmm are different particles; de Resende’s reading of it as “spin-14\tfrac1441​” (p. 17) says more than it does. A phase other than +1+1+1 for one particle taken around another is what makes eee and mmm anyons, which two space dimensions allow (Symmetries, L15, a starred reading).

14.5 L=3L=3L=3 and suppression

Take L=3L=3L=3: 181818 qubits and d=3d=3d=3. Look at XXX errors; ZZZ errors are the same on the dual lattice. An error X(a)X(a)X(a) shows the mmm’s at the ends of its dual strings. Least-weight decoding applies an X(b)X(b)X(b) of least weight with the same syndrome. Then X(a)X(b)=X(a+b)X(a)X(b)=X(a+b)X(a)X(b)=X(a+b), with a+ba+ba+b the edges in one of aaa, bbb but not both, has no syndrome: a+ba+ba+b is a dual loop. The correction succeeds if X(a+b)X(a+b)X(a+b) is a product of stars, which acts as 1\Id1 on the code, and otherwise leaves a logical error.

Every single XXX error is corrected: on L=3L=3L=3 two faces share at most one edge, so the syndrome of one edge, the two faces at it, is that of no other single edge, and the decoder undoes the error itself. Two errors can defeat it: XXX on v(0,1)v(0,1)v(0,1) and v(0,2)v(0,2)v(0,2) (dashed) shows mmm’s at p(0,0)p(0,0)p(0,0) and p(0,2)p(0,2)p(0,2); p(0,1)p(0,1)p(0,1), which holds both, is not violated. The least-weight correction is the one edge between p(0,2)p(0,2)p(0,2) and p(0,0)p(0,0)p(0,0), across the glued sides: XXX on v(0,0)v(0,0)v(0,0) (dotted). The three make X(x2)=Xˉ2X(x_2)=\bar X_2X(x2​)=Xˉ2​, a logical error: the decoder closed the loop the short way round, and the error had gone the long way. With d=3d=3d=3, one error is guaranteed to be corrected, not two. Of the 153153153 pairs of XXX errors, 181818 fail this way, the three pairs on each of the six straight dual loops; 818181 have one least-weight correction, which succeeds, and 545454 have several, which differ by stars and all succeed: errors that differ by a star act alike on the code, which is degenerate for them (§11.6).

“Protected” does not mean that failures are absent; it means that they are suppressed as LLL grows, under a stated model of noise and decoding. Dennis et al. take independent XXX and ZZZ errors on each qubit, each with probability ppp per round. With perfect syndromes, a round of errors is corrected with a failure probability that tends to 000 as LLL grows when ppp is below about 10.5%10.5\%10.5% with least-weight decoding, and below about 10.9%10.9\%10.9% with optimal decoding (values of Kawashima and Aoki, and of Honecker et al., which they quote, pp. 17–18). When each syndrome bit is also wrong with probability ppp, independently across checks and rounds and of the data errors, they decode the history of TTT rounds of syndromes at once, by least weight and with exact classical processing, and prove that for p<1.14%p<1.14\%p<1.14%, a lower bound on the threshold, the failure probability decreases exponentially with LLL, for TTT growing no faster than a polynomial in LLL (pp. 10–11, 18–20).

14.6 The third answer

Protection can grow with the size of the system. Each star and each plaquette of the toric code acts on four qubits, whatever LLL, while every logical operator goes around the torus: d=Ld=Ld=L. An error does harm only when its strings, with the decoder’s, close a loop around the torus, and below threshold the probability of that falls exponentially with LLL, under the models above (Kitaev, p. 5; Dennis et al., p. 20). This completes the third answer: errors are discrete and correctable (Lectures 10–11), stabilizer codes give them a working form (Lecture 12), a protected computation needs Clifford gadgets, magic states and a threshold (Lecture 13), and topology makes protection grow with size. The anyons here are abelian: taking eee around mmm multiplies the state by a number.

Summary

  • The toric code: qubits on the 2L22L^{2}2L2 edges, commuting stars of XXX’s and plaquettes of ZZZ’s; k=2k=2k=2.
  • Loops: contractible loops and dual loops are products of stabilizers; the straight loops around the torus are logical; d=Ld=Ld=L (cited).
  • Anyons: errors are strings with eee’s or mmm’s at their ends; an eee taken around an mmm gives −1-1−1, and Xˉa\bar X_aXˉa​, Zˉa\bar Z_aZˉa​ anticommute, as their strings cross once.
  • Protection: on L=3L=3L=3 one error is corrected, two can make a logical error; below threshold, failure falls exponentially with LLL, under a stated model.

2 Which argument proves that the stars and plaquettes of the toric code commute, that −1∉S-\Id\notin S−1∈/S, and that the code has dimension 444?

6 What does “protected” mean for the toric code in Lecture 14?

Here is the hint — try once more. Is any statement about protection in the lecture free of a model? And is 1.14%1.14\%1.14% a threshold or a bound? No size corrects every error: on L=3L=3L=3 two errors on a straight dual loop already defeat the decoder, and on any lattice an error that closes, with its correction, a loop around the torus leaves a logical error. Protection means suppression with LLL, and it needs a model: independent errors of probability ppp, a decoder, and, if the syndrome bits fail too, their independence and a storage time polynomial in LLL. The numbers are not one threshold: about 10.5%10.5\%10.5% (least weight) and 10.9%10.9\%10.9% (optimal) are values that Dennis et al. quote for perfect syndromes, and 1.14%1.14\%1.14% is a lower bound that they prove with faulty syndromes; above it they prove nothing.
